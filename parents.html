FOR PARENTS · GEORGIA GRADE 3 ENGLISH LANGUAGE ARTS

What our apps teach, and what they don't yet.

Every Georgia grade 3 ELA standard, matched against what Sentence Sense, Word Workshop and Study Scoop actually contain. Gaps are shown on purpose, so you know what to practice somewhere else.

Checked October 2026 · The official Milestones blueprint weights were not available to confirm.

The short version

  • TaughtCovered well: grammar and mechanics. Verbs, verb tense (including irregular past like went), pronouns, subject-verb agreement, capital letters, end marks, apostrophes, and commas for yes/no and names. 17 of the 41 grade 3 grammar-chart skills are taught.
  • PartlyPartly covered: spelling and weekly vocabulary (the class lists, not word-meaning skills like prefixes or context clues), sentence structure (fragments and run-ons, but not complex sentences), and adjectives and adverbs (found, not yet used to improve writing).
  • Not yetBiggest gaps: reading comprehension and the source-based opinion essay. None of the 29 grade 3 reading and writing (Texts) standards is practiced in these apps yet, and that is where most of the Milestones points are.

How we checked this

  • Standards: taken from Georgia's K-12 ELA Standards (approved 2023, used from 2025-26), grade 3 only, including every grade 3 row of the Grammar, Usage & Mechanics chart (rows marked "not yet introduced" for grade 3 are left out). Wording on this page is a faithful paraphrase; the official document is linked below.
  • Apps: we read each app's lessons and question banks, not its descriptions. A standard is marked "taught" only when we found lessons or practice questions for it, and the "Where to practice" column says how many. Sentence Sense questions carry standard tags; for its older Verb, Subject, Noun and Adjective lessons we matched the content ourselves (marked "inferred").
  • Sentence Sense results describe Build 1.5.0, released October 19, 2026. Word Workshop and Study Scoop spelling and vocabulary use your child's weekly class lists, so what they cover changes week to week.
  • The test: grade 3 ELA has three sections. Section 1: read 2–3 sources, answer 3–4 questions, then write an opinion essay scored out of 8 points (Purpose & Organization 0–3, Evidence & Elaboration 0–3, Language Usage & Conventions 0–2). Sections 2 and 3 use multiple-choice, multi-select and technology-enhanced items (drag-and-drop, hot text, drop-down). The "How the Milestones tests it" column is our best judgment, not an official map.
